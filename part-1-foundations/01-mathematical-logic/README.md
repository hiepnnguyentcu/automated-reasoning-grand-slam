# Chapter 1: Mathematical Logic

Part 1: Foundations.

## Objective

Reason precisely about propositional and first-order logic and prove their central metatheorems.

## Proof of mastery

Prove the completeness of a propositional proof system by hand in a closed-book exam.

## Topics

| Topic | Objective |
|---|---|
| [Propositional Logic](propositional-logic/) | Define the syntax and semantics of propositional logic and decide validity, satisfiability and entailment by hand. |
| [Proof Systems](proof-systems/) | Derive formulas in natural deduction, sequent calculus and resolution and explain how the systems differ. |
| [Soundness and Completeness](soundness-and-completeness/) | Prove the soundness and completeness of a propositional proof system from memory. |
| [First-Order Logic](first-order-logic/) | Formalize statements in first-order logic and evaluate formulas in a given structure. |
| [Normal Forms and Skolemization](normal-forms-and-skolemization/) | Convert formulas to negation, prenex, conjunctive and Skolem normal forms and state what each conversion preserves. |
| [First-Order Metatheory](first-order-metatheory/) | State and apply completeness, compactness, Löwenheim-Skolem and Herbrand's theorem. |
| [First-Order Theories](first-order-theories/) | Axiomatize common theories and decide whether a formula is valid modulo a theory. |

Theory covers the lesson, worked examples, common mistakes and self-check at about 30 words per minute, which leaves time to work through the proofs. Exercises allow 5 minutes each plus 1 minute for every 8 words of the published solution, which covers attempting the exercise closed-book and checking your answer.

These are planning estimates calculated from the length of each lesson, and they have not been checked against anyone's actual study time. They exclude the listed materials, revision and the proof of mastery.
