# Chapter 2: Decidability and Complexity

Part 1: Foundations.

## Objective

Determine how hard a reasoning problem is and what that implies for the choice of algorithm.

## Proof of mastery

Classify unseen reasoning problems by decidability and complexity and justify each answer with a reduction.

## Topics

| Topic | Objective |
|---|---|
| [Computability](computability/) | Prove undecidability results using Turing machines, the halting problem and Rice's theorem. |
| [Reductions and NP-Completeness](reductions-and-np-completeness/) | Prove the Cook-Levin theorem and construct polynomial reductions to and from SAT. |
| [Space and the Polynomial Hierarchy](space-and-the-polynomial-hierarchy/) | Place QBF, model checking and related problems in PSPACE and the polynomial hierarchy. |
| [Undecidability of Logic and Incompleteness](undecidability-of-logic-and-incompleteness/) | Explain the undecidability of first-order validity and the statements and limits of Gödel's theorems. |
| [Decidable Fragments](decidable-fragments/) | Identify decidable logical fragments, including Presburger arithmetic and effectively propositional logic, and their complexity. |
| [Proof Complexity](proof-complexity/) | Explain why resolution proofs of the pigeonhole principle are exponential and what this means for CDCL solvers. |
